MIStraLab operates across multiple fields of materials science and metallurgy, with broad diversity in research themes. Current studies cover processing routes, material structure, and structure-property relationships, supported by data-driven methodologies. The group also pursues emerging approaches, new models, and expanding topics, and selected examples are provided below.

## Data-driven Materials Discovery
Machine learning and materials informatics are used to identify promising candidates from broad composition and process design spaces. Predictive models connect composition, processing, and target properties to reduce trial-and-error in laboratory work. This workflow supports faster decision-making for experiments and more efficient material development cycles.

## Hydrometallurgical Recycling Process Optimization
Data-driven methods are applied to optimize complex hydrometallurgical recycling routes and improve selective recovery of valuable elements. Process data, chemical understanding, and model-based optimization are combined to increase yield while lowering operational costs. The overall aim is to support cleaner and more sustainable resource recovery technologies.

## Computational Thermodynamics and CALPHAD
Computational thermodynamics and CALPHAD tools are integrated with data-driven workflows to predict phase stability and process behavior. These models help define reliable composition ranges and processing windows before extensive experimental campaigns. The resulting framework supports alloy design, heat-treatment planning, and risk reduction during scale-up.

## AI for Additive Manufacturing
AI models are developed to connect process parameters with melt-pool dynamics, defect formation, and final microstructure in additive manufacturing. These relationships are used to improve process stability and guide parameter selection for better part quality. This approach helps reduce costly trial runs and supports robust manufacturing performance.

## Electronic Structure, DFT, and Property Prediction
Electronic-structure-informed machine learning and density functional theory (DFT) are used together to estimate key material properties and identify hidden structure-property trends. By combining first-principles descriptors with statistical learning, faster screening of functional materials becomes possible for targeted applications. These methods also improve interpretability when working with complex and high-dimensional materials datasets.
